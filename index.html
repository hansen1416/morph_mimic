Abstract

Physics-based motion imitation must accommodate both the diversity of human movement and differences in body geometry and dynamics. We construct a text-associated, multi-morphology motion corpus from AMASS-derived HumanML3D clips using HUMOS, pairing shape-specific reference motions with matching SMPL-based simulation assets. The corpus contains 20,951 clip identities, including mirrored clips, across 128 body configurations, yielding 2,681,728 generated variants. Source captions remain associated metadata rather than controller inputs. A contact-aware kinematic refinement procedure reduces reference artifacts while retaining explicit limits on pose and root changes. We train one morphology-conditioned policy directly on the refined multi-shape references, using temporal slot/type attention and a streamed training pool. Deterministic, clip-grouped splits and morphology-matched evaluation distinguish held-out motion tracking from training-pool monitoring. Offline evaluation of the selected checkpoint reports 98.47% success on the 1,048-clip validation split and 99.14% on the independent 1,048-clip test split, each with one selected body configuration per clip and a capped horizon; these are trained-body, one-shape-per-clip results, not all-shape or unseen-body tests. An eight-run grid of four temporal architectures crossed with refined and unrefined references, on a 150-clip development set, selects temporal slot/type attention without adaLN-Zero. Residual failures on both splits are analysed against inherited captions and concentrate on ground-contact transitions and whole-body acrobatics. A separate analysis asks whether body shape and motion type jointly set physical control demand, replaying the trained policy across all 19,200 (clip, shape) pairs of the 150-clip development set: after correcting a normalization artifact that would otherwise have produced a spurious positive result, we find no statistically detectable evidence that highly dynamic motions are more shape-sensitive than quasi-static ones for this checkpoint. A further test on 32 held-out body shapes finds 28 of 32 track at trained-body levels with no decline across distance from training, while the remaining 4 fail severely in a gender-flip pattern consistent with an asset artifact rather than a genuine limit, reported as an open anomaly rather than averaged away.

Pipeline

How one shared policy ends up controlling 128 different body shapes: matched data construction, then a training loop where the body shape, its actuation, and its reference all stay consistent per environment.

Dataset & Code

The full multi-morphology motion corpus is derived from AMASS via HumanML3D clip identities and HUMOS shape-conditioned generation. Per AMASS's license, the derived motion data is not redistributed directly here — the full construction pipeline is released as code, so anyone with their own AMASS access can regenerate the identical corpus.
