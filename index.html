Abstract

Animating digital humans with different body proportions requires references consistent with their geometry and a controller that tracks them in physics. We study shared tracking, unseen-body transfer, and network-design trade-offs using one morphology-conditioned temporal controller. A paired corpus links 20,951 motion identities to body-conditioned references and matching simulation assets for 128 body configurations; training uses 18,855 identities across all bodies. The controller achieves 99.14% tracking success on 1,048 held-out motions, with one selected trained body per clip. On 233 training-split clips replayed across each of two 128-body unseen panels, success is 97.28% inside the training coefficient range and 97.15% outside it. These averages coexist with individual-body success as low as 3.0% and 13.3%, respectively. A later checkpoint improves outside-panel success but introduces substantial individual regressions. Three-seed comparisons of temporal multilayer perceptron and attention architectures reveal trade-offs between tracking and smoothness. An offline reference audit measures reduced stance-foot sliding and below-floor body centres with small pose changes, at the cost of increased reference jerk. These reference measurements are distinct from simulated tracking accuracy and smoothness; rollout sliding and penetration have not been quantified. The results demonstrate strong average shared tracking and transfer within the evaluated domain, while leaving all-body held-out-motion coverage, the causal contribution of explicit morphology inputs, and severe failure mechanisms unresolved.

Contributions and evaluation

  1. Paired corpus and workflow: 20,951 clip identities on 128 body configurations (2,681,728 clip/body variants), each identity preserved from generation through simulation and replay.
  2. Shared controller: one temporal slot/type-attention controller with streamed residency, trained directly on 18,855 clips across all 128 bodies.
  3. Exposure-separated evaluation: 1,048 clip-disjoint test clips on trained bodies, and 233 training-split clips replayed on 128 unseen inside-range and 128 unseen outside-range bodies (29,824 clip/body pairs per panel), retaining every body and failure.
  4. Design comparison and reference audit: three-seed comparisons of four network architectures, and a reference-refinement audit of 19,200 development references (150 clips × 128 bodies).

Tracking and unseen-body results

Success requires peak mean body-position error no greater than 0.5 m over the evaluated horizon. This is a tracking criterion, not a certification of visual realism. Unseen-body results were recorded on 9 October 2026.

Mean reference changes are 0.12° in body rotation and 0.008 m in horizontal root position; the cross-shape knee-range variance ratio is 0.94. Refinement reduces the measured reference artifacts but increases reference jerk. Below-floor body centres are not mesh-penetration measurements. Raw reference jerk and the simulated normalized-jerk score have different definitions.

The held-out test measures simulated body-position error of 0.0676 m, rotation error of 0.1723 rad and normalized jerk of 1,050.9. Simulated sliding and penetration have not been quantified. All-body held-out-motion coverage and controlled reference-versus-simulation visual comparisons remain open.

Published capabilities and our contribution

These sources describe capabilities and evaluation scope, not directly comparable performance. No external tracker has been rerun on our matched references, assets and protocol.

Primary-source capability comparison
SystemPublished capabilityRelation to this study
PHCReference tracking and recovery; sampled SMPL shape/gender variation described.Quantitative motion comparisons use the mean neutral body. Shape support should not be confused with a systematic inside/outside unseen-body benchmark.
ASEReusable adversarial skill embeddings and downstream task control.Exact clip tracking is not its objective; the published study does not report our shape-conditioned tracking panel.
ProtoMotions 3Framework documentation covers tracking, distributed training, retargeting and multiple character configurations.A software capability source, not a matched benchmark. Our simulation, PPO and motion-imitation infrastructure is inherited from ProtoMotions.
Won & LeeParametric physics-based control across height, weight and proportion changes.Direct prior body-control capability; our contribution is not the invention of morphology conditioning.
HUMOSIdentity-conditioned motion generation with physical-consistency objectives.Our pretrained reference generator, rather than a directly comparable online feedback tracker.

Our additions are paired corpus assembly and identity checks, reference processing and its fidelity audit, the evaluated temporal controller configuration, streamed clip residency, and evaluation that separates motion exposure from body exposure. SMPL/SMPLSim, HUMOS and ProtoMotions provide the underlying body models, generation and learning infrastructure.

Pipeline

How one shared policy ends up controlling 128 different body shapes: matched data construction, then a training loop where the body shape, its actuation, and its reference all stay consistent per environment.

Dataset & Code

The full multi-morphology motion corpus is derived from AMASS via HumanML3D clip identities and HUMOS shape-conditioned generation. Derived motion tensors are not redistributed here; use remains subject to the source datasets' access and licensing conditions. The research code, the simulation body models and the evaluated checkpoint are linked below. Reproducing the evaluations requires the motion corpus, or references regenerated with the released pipeline.

  • Code & pipeline: github.com/hansen1416/morph_mimic (version v1.0)
  • Body-shape definitions & evaluated simulation assets: included in the code repository under protomotions/data/assets: the 128 trained bodies, the two 128-body unseen panels and their shape coefficients. The body models are derived from SMPL and released with attribution (CC BY 4.0).
  • Trained checkpoint: huggingface.co/YugoAmaryl/morph-mimic (epoch 28,170, CC BY-NC 4.0). The epoch-34,000 checkpoint is not released.
  • Motion data and evaluation archive: the motion corpus is not distributed; the released pipeline can regenerate references from the source datasets. No unrestricted derived-data release is claimed.
